# Ragi (Finger Millet) Cultivation Guide — Karnataka

## Overview
Ragi (Eleusine coracana) is the most important millet crop in Karnataka, grown across 1.2 million hectares. It is a staple food crop, drought-tolerant, and highly nutritious. Karnataka is the largest ragi producer in India, accounting for about 58% of the national production. The crop is deeply embedded in the food culture of Karnataka, used for mudde (ragi ball), roti, porridge, and malt.

## Suitable Conditions
- Climate: Warm and moderately humid. Grows well in both Kharif and summer seasons.
- Temperature: 20-30°C optimal. Can tolerate up to 35°C.
- Rainfall: 500-1000mm. Grows well under rainfed conditions.
- Altitude: Up to 2000m above sea level.
- Soil: Well-drained red loamy soils, red sandy loam, and laterite soils. pH 5.0-8.0.
- Best districts in Karnataka: Tumkur, Hassan, Mandya, Bangalore Rural, Ramanagara, Chitradurga, Kolar, Mysuru.

## Recommended Varieties
- GPU-28: Most popular variety. 115-120 days. Blast resistant. Yield: 30-35 quintals/hectare.
- GPU-67: 110-115 days. Good for dry areas. Yield: 25-30 quintals/hectare.
- MR-1: Early maturing (100-105 days). Good for late sowing.
- GPU-48: 110-115 days. High calcium content.
- KMR-340 (Indaf-9): 115-120 days. Good for irrigated conditions.
- ML-365: 110 days. Blast resistant. Suitable for both rainfed and irrigated.

## Land Preparation
1. Plough the field 2-3 times to get a fine tilth
2. Apply 5-7.5 tonnes of well-decomposed FYM per acre during the last ploughing
3. Level the field properly for uniform water distribution
4. Form ridges and furrows at 30 cm spacing for transplanted ragi

## Seed Treatment and Sowing
- Seed rate: 4-5 kg per acre for transplanting, 8-10 kg per acre for direct sowing
- Treat seeds with Carbendazim 2g per kg seed (for blast prevention)
- Or treat with Trichoderma viride at 4g per kg seed (organic option)
- Nursery sowing: Sow seeds in raised nursery beds, transplant 21-25 day old seedlings
- Direct sowing: Broadcast or line sow after the onset of monsoon rains
- Kharif season: Sow in June-July
- Summer season: Sow in January-February (under irrigation)

## Spacing
- Transplanted: 22.5 × 10 cm (row × plant spacing) — 2-3 seedlings per hill
- Direct sown: Thin to 10 cm between plants after 15-20 days

## Fertilizer Management
- Basal dose: 10 kg Nitrogen + 20 kg P2O5 + 10 kg K2O per acre at transplanting
- Top dressing: 10 kg Nitrogen per acre at 30 days after transplanting
- For organic farming: Apply 2 tonnes of vermicompost + 50 kg Neem cake per acre
- Zinc deficiency is common in red soils — apply ZnSO4 at 10 kg per acre if needed

## Water Management
- Ragi is drought-tolerant but responds well to irrigation at critical stages
- Critical stages: Tillering (30-35 days) and Flowering (55-65 days)
- Life-saving irrigation during prolonged dry spells increases yield significantly
- Avoid waterlogging — ragi does not tolerate standing water
- Drain excess water within 24 hours after heavy rains

## Harvesting
- Harvest when 80% of the earheads turn brown and grains are hard
- Cut the earheads, dry for 2-3 days, and thresh
- Ragi can be stored for 5+ years without insect damage (natural pest resistance)
- Clean and grade grains before storage
- Store in jute bags or metal bins in a dry, ventilated room
