# Paddy (Rice) Pest and Disease Management Guide

## Common Diseases

### Blast Disease (Pyricularia oryzae)
Symptoms: Spindle-shaped lesions on leaves with brown/gray centers and dark brown margins. Neck blast causes the panicle to break at the node. Node blast causes dark brown spots at the nodes. Most severe during cloudy, humid weather with cool nights (20-25°C).
Management:
- Spray Tricyclazole 75% WP at 0.6g per liter of water at tillering stage
- Or spray Isoprothiolane 40% EC at 1.5ml per liter
- Use blast-resistant varieties like Rasi, IR-64, or KRH-4
- Avoid excessive nitrogen fertilization — split nitrogen into 3 doses
- Maintain recommended spacing (20×15 cm for transplanted rice)
- Drain water from fields during blast infection period
- For exact dosage, consult your nearest KVK

### Bacterial Leaf Blight (Xanthomonas oryzae)
Symptoms: Water-soaked to yellowish stripes on leaf blades starting from the tip. Lesions spread along leaf margins. Leaves dry out from the tip downward. Milky bacterial ooze on cut leaf surfaces in the morning.
Management:
- No effective chemical control — prevention is key
- Use resistant varieties like Improved Samba Mahsuri (ISM)
- Avoid clipping of seedling tips during transplanting
- Drain excess water from fields
- Spray Streptocycline (streptomycin sulphate) at 0.5g + Copper Oxychloride at 3g per liter as preventive
- Avoid movement through infected fields during wet conditions

### Sheath Blight (Rhizoctonia solani)
Symptoms: Oval to irregular greenish-gray lesions on leaf sheaths near water level. Lesions enlarge and spread upward. In severe cases, the entire plant may lodge (fall over). Common in dense plantings with high humidity.
Management:
- Spray Hexaconazole 5% SC at 2ml per liter or Propiconazole 25% EC at 1ml per liter
- Maintain wider spacing to improve air circulation
- Drain water periodically to reduce humidity at the base
- Apply potassium fertilizer to strengthen plant resistance
- Use Trichoderma-enriched FYM in the field

## Common Pests

### Stem Borer (Scirpophaga incertulas)
Symptoms: Dead hearts in vegetative stage — the central shoot dries and can be pulled out easily. White ears (empty panicles) at flowering stage. Tiny bore holes at the base of affected tillers.
Management:
- Set up pheromone traps at 5 per acre to monitor moth emergence
- Release egg parasitoid Trichogramma japonicum at 1 lakh per acre
- Apply Cartap Hydrochloride 4% granules at 8 kg per acre in standing water
- Remove and destroy stubbles after harvest to reduce carryover population
- Clip the tip of seedlings before transplanting to remove egg masses

### Brown Plant Hopper (BPH)
Symptoms: Hopper burn — round yellow patches in the field that turn brown and die. Insects are found at the base of tillers near water surface. Sooty mold growth due to honeydew secretion. Plants fall over in severe cases.
Management:
- Avoid indiscriminate insecticide use — it kills BPH natural enemies
- Drain the field for 3-4 days if BPH population is noticed
- Spray Pymetrozine 50% WG at 0.6g per liter (selective against hoppers)
- Avoid excessive nitrogen — use balanced NPK fertilization
- Use resistant varieties where available
- Light traps attract and kill adult BPH at night

### Leaf Folder (Cnaphalocrocis medinalis)
Symptoms: Leaves folded longitudinally with silken threads. Larva feeds inside the folded leaf, creating white streaks. Severe feeding reduces photosynthesis and grain filling.
Management:
- Spray Chlorantraniliprole 18.5% SC at 0.3ml per liter
- Or use Flubendiamide 39.35% SC at 0.2ml per liter
- Hand-picking is effective in small plots
- Maintain field hygiene — remove weeds from bunds
- Avoid continuous flooding — alternate wetting and drying
