# Tomato Disease and Pest Management Guide

## Common Diseases

### Early Blight (Alternaria solani)
Symptoms: Dark brown to black concentric ring-shaped spots on older leaves. Starts from lower leaves and progresses upward. Leaves turn yellow and drop. Fruit shows dark sunken spots near the stem.
Management:
- Remove and destroy infected leaves immediately
- Spray Mancozeb 75% WP at 2.5g per liter of water at 10-day intervals
- Apply Copper Oxychloride 50% WP at 3g per liter as preventive spray
- Practice crop rotation — don't grow tomato in the same field for 2-3 seasons
- Use disease-resistant varieties like Arka Rakshak or Arka Samrat

### Late Blight (Phytophthora infestans)
Symptoms: Water-soaked patches on leaves that turn brown rapidly. White fungal growth on the underside of leaves in humid conditions. Fruits show brown, greasy-looking patches. Entire plant can die within days in severe cases.
Management:
- Spray Metalaxyl + Mancozeb (Ridomil Gold) at 2.5g per liter immediately
- Improve air circulation by wider spacing and staking
- Avoid overhead irrigation — use drip irrigation
- Remove and burn severely affected plants
- For exact dosage, consult your nearest KVK (Krishi Vigyan Kendra)

### Bacterial Wilt (Ralstonia solanacearum)
Symptoms: Sudden permanent wilting of the entire plant without yellowing. When the stem is cut and placed in water, milky white bacterial ooze flows out. More common in hot, humid weather and waterlogged soils.
Management:
- No chemical cure — prevention is the only option
- Use wilt-resistant varieties or grafted seedlings
- Treat soil with Trichoderma viride at 2.5 kg per acre mixed with well-rotted FYM
- Practice crop rotation with non-solanaceous crops (cereals, legumes)
- Ensure good soil drainage — raised beds are recommended

### Leaf Curl Virus (Tomato Leaf Curl Virus - ToLCV)
Symptoms: Leaves curl upward and inward. Stunted plant growth. Leaves become thick, leathery, and brittle. Yellow margins on curled leaves. Severely reduced fruit set.
Management:
- Transmitted by whiteflies — control the vector
- Spray Imidacloprid 17.8% SL at 0.3ml per liter to control whiteflies
- Use yellow sticky traps at 12 per acre to monitor and trap whiteflies
- Remove and destroy infected plants immediately
- Use virus-resistant varieties like Arka Ananya
- Install insect-proof net (40 mesh) in nurseries

## Common Pests

### Fruit Borer (Helicoverpa armigera)
Symptoms: Circular bore holes in fruits. Larva feeds inside the fruit causing rotting. Frass (excreta) visible near the bore hole.
Management:
- Install pheromone traps at 5 per acre to monitor adult moths
- Spray Neem oil (Azadirachtin 1500 ppm) at 5ml per liter as organic option
- Hand-pick and destroy affected fruits
- Spray Emamectin Benzoate 5% SG at 0.4g per liter for severe infestation
- Release Trichogramma parasitoid wasps at 1 lakh per acre (bio-control)

### Whitefly (Bemisia tabaci)
Symptoms: Tiny white flying insects on the underside of leaves. Honeydew secretion leading to sooty mold. Transmits leaf curl virus.
Management:
- Spray Neem oil at 5ml per liter as first line of defense
- Use yellow sticky traps at 12 per acre
- Spray Thiamethoxam 25% WG at 0.3g per liter for heavy infestation
- Avoid excessive nitrogen fertilization which attracts whiteflies
- Intercrop with marigold to repel whiteflies

### Thrips
Symptoms: Silver-colored streaks on leaves. Leaf edges curl downward. Brown spots on fruits. Stunted growth in seedlings.
Management:
- Spray Spinosad 45% SC at 0.3ml per liter (organic-certified option)
- Blue sticky traps are effective for monitoring thrips
- Avoid water stress — thrips attack more in dry conditions
- Mulching helps maintain soil moisture and reduce thrips populations
